## Disclosure status

`docs/security-audit/findings.md` states that the public ledger omits reproduction payloads and
exploit mechanics for unremediated medium-or-higher findings. This document and its continuations
carry exactly that detail, so **this branch must not be merged to the published site while any High
or Medium finding here remains open**.

That gate is now satisfied. Both High findings (`MCW-V09-101`, `MCW-V09-102`) and all nine Medium
findings (`MCW-V09-103`..`111`) are Resolved, verified against the working tree by a final
integration review that re-read every fix and executed harnesses against the real `src/` modules
rather than trusting the round-1 and round-2 status claims. One Low finding, `MCW-V09-129`, remains
partially remediated: its wording and its residual are recorded in full at
[low findings](../v0.9-deep-tail/#mcw-v09-129) and in the source module's own doc comment. A Low
carrying a stated residual does not hold publication, because nothing withheld from the public
ledger applies to it — the ledger only withholds detail for unremediated medium-or-higher entries.

## Executive summary

This is a post-release adversarial audit of the complete v0.9 product surface, commissioned after
the `v0.9.0` release assurance record (`MCW-V09-001`..`006`). It is deliberately broader and
stricter than a release review: every trust boundary, every product line, and the four dimensions
named by the maintainer — memory, plaintext lifetime, entropy, and vault security — were swept
independently, and every finding was then handed to an adversary instructed to refute it. Of 127
raw findings, 50 did not survive that challenge. The 31 recorded here survived both adversarial
review and a lead-auditor re-reading.

Two findings were High. `MCW-V09-101` was the most serious: at the baseline, SNIP-12 revision 0 was
accepted with no type-graph validation, so a site could obtain a signature whose message hash is
byte-identical to a different, attacker-chosen struct. This was reproduced end to end against the
shipped `typedDataHash`, not merely reasoned about. `MCW-V09-102` let whoever answers a multisig
user's RPC choose which proposal that user's confirmation lands on. Both are now closed.

The wallet's core custody was sound at the baseline. Entropy is clean, every memory defect is
self-healing under MV3 eviction, and the encrypted store's cryptography is correctly constructed.
The weakest dimension was plaintext lifetime: the advertised inactivity auto-lock was defeated three
independent ways at once, so in normal use it effectively did not fire.

Everything below describes the `bd9fcbb` baseline. Each finding's own entry records what shipped.
The remediation is summarised in the [findings ledger](../../findings/); the one entry that is not
fully closed is `MCW-V09-129`.

## Verification method

Findings were produced by parallel domain auditors, each followed immediately by an independent
adversarial reviewer instructed to disprove them and to default to refutation when uncertain. Only
findings a second agent reproduced from source survived. Four cross-cutting passes then covered
type and validator consistency, a product-line guard-parity matrix, secret-lifetime synthesis, and
a completeness critic tasked with finding what the domain sweep missed.

Every High and Medium finding was re-read by the lead auditor against the cited source. Six were
additionally confirmed by executing throwaway harnesses importing the real `src/` modules: the
SNIP-12 type collision, the displayed-but-unhashed domain field, the approval-queue blanking, the
key-boundary scan coverage, the token-id split, and the swap amount rejection. The repository was
not modified during the audit.

One calibration note is recorded deliberately. The automated synthesis quoted specific hash
constants for `MCW-V09-101` that did not reproduce; the underlying mechanism was independently
re-derived and confirmed with a fresh construction. Numeric claims in automated output are
hypotheses until executed.

## Verdicts on the four named dimensions

**Entropy — sound.** No finding. Every random value derives from a CSPRNG, lengths are adequate,
and no capability token — approval id, preparation id, provider request id, Ledger sign id — is
guessable or grants authority on its own. This is the wallet's strongest dimension.

**Memory — sound, with bounded defects.** No memory finding exceeds Low. Every unbounded structure
lives in the MV3 service worker, which Chrome evicts, so the failure mode is a self-healing restart
rather than corruption or disclosure. No vault object is ever cached, preparation stores are
FIFO-bounded and content-addressed, and the Ledger sign queue is both timeout- and generation-bound.
The one path worth closing is `MCW-V09-115`.

**Vault security — sound core, peripheral gaps.** PBKDF2-SHA-256 at 600k iterations, AES-256-GCM
with scope-bound `additionalData`, a fresh 16-byte salt and 12-byte IV per write, and legacy
acceptance restricted to the single exact value `310_000` with transparent re-encryption. The gaps
sit around the vault rather than in it: `MCW-V09-107`, `MCW-V09-114`, and `MCW-V09-128`.

**Plaintext lifetime — was the weakest dimension.** Custody is well built: secrets are confined to
whitelisted modules, both storage areas are locked to trusted contexts as a fatal startup
prerequisite, the reveal path re-demands the passphrase rather than accepting the session, and
viewing keys are derived per use behind a lazy callback. But at the baseline the control bounding how
long the root secret lives was defeated three ways at once (`MCW-V09-105`), and an in-flight vault
signature completed after an explicit user Lock (`MCW-V09-113`). Both are closed, and expiry is now
driven by a `chrome.alarms` wake rather than by whoever happens to read the session next.

## Residual boundaries

- The aggregate gate could not be executed end to end during this audit. `vendor:krusty`,
  `vendor:multisig`, and `vendor:privacy` require network access plus `wasm-pack`, `scarb`, and
  Node.js 24, so `check:krusty`, `check:multisig`, `check:privacy`, `build`, and `test:e2e` were
  not run. All verification came from targeted harnesses importing real modules.
- No browser observation was performed. MV3 service-worker eviction, `chrome.storage.session`
  behaviour, side-panel activation semantics, and held-response delivery across eviction were
  reasoned from source and manifest only.
- Vendored artifacts were trusted as shipped. The Krusty KMS WASM, the multisig Cairo class, and
  the pinned privacy SDK were not analysed; `MCW-V09-127` concerns the absence of provenance
  verification, not their contents.
- Cryptographic review is boundary-level. The `starknet.js` Pedersen and Poseidon implementations
  were not audited beyond the revision-0 `encodeType` path exploited in `MCW-V09-101`.
- At the baseline, roughly 86 privileged-only handlers were reviewed by reading rather than by
  execution, because the key-boundary scan that would exercise them was itself partially inert
  (`MCW-V09-111`). That scan now drives all 104 catalog entries against a live handler, so the
  boundary no longer applies to the remediated tree.
- This is a scoped adversarial review, not an absence-of-defects guarantee, and not a substitute
  for an independent external audit.
